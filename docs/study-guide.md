# How to study and revise

Start at [F01](../lessons/01-foundations/F01-programs.md), or take the [diagnostic](../interviews/diagnostic.md) first. The [curriculum](../curriculum/README.md) supplies the exact sequence and prerequisites. A lesson is available when marked ready; you are ready only when you can demonstrate its outcome.

## A daily 60-minute session

1. **10 minutes:** answer due recall prompts closed-book, including one weak topic. Draw or trace from memory.
2. **15 minutes:** study one new mechanism, then close the lesson and explain it in your own words.
3. **20 minutes:** implement/debug its exercise without copying. Write edge cases before checking the solution.
4. **10 minutes:** give a spoken answer and handle a follow-up from the [question system](../interviews/README.md).
5. **5 minutes:** record seven skill ratings and concrete evidence in [revision](../revision/README.md). Choose the next lesson from prerequisites and weaknesses.

For a 30-minute day, do recall, one smaller implementation, and a spoken explanation. Avoid advancing several lessons just because reading felt easy. For a project session, spend the implementation block on one acceptance criterion, then discuss its trade-off.

## Mastery and skipping

Score recognition, explanation, implementation, debugging, application, trade-off reasoning, and interview performance separately, 0–3. Level 2 means independent and correct; level 3 means an unfamiliar variant is handled. A missing implementation or security boundary cannot be offset by fluent delivery. Demonstrate every relevant dimension at level 2+, repeat after a delay, and pass the module's project/assessment before calling it mastered.

If the diagnostic proves a skill, skip introductory reading but still attempt its failure case and delayed check. If a prerequisite fails, repair it before advancing dependent lessons. Do not use the existence of an authored document as evidence of coverage depth beyond what its exercises demonstrate.

## Interview revision mode

Use the selected [role track](../interviews/tracks.md), comparison sheet, due questions, and one timed mock. Answer first, inspect the answer second, and immediately retest a weak point with a different example. Spend most time on an explicit weakness, not on collecting more question counts. Rehearse projects with requirements, your implementation, a failure found, a measured result, and a trade-off. State unimplemented extensions honestly.

Use AI after your own attempt to find counterexamples and challenge assumptions. Verify APIs against the pinned toolchain and inspect tests independently. Keep your first attempt, the change, and the evidence; be able to reproduce the final reasoning without AI.
